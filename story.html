Our story

A warm room
at the top
of the road.

Inuvik sits at 68° north, at the end of the Dempster Highway, on the edge of the Mackenzie Delta. Winters hit forty below. The sun doesn't rise for weeks. And on MacKenzie Road, one kitchen has been feeding this town through all of it.

"While the pandemic forced much of the hospitality industry to shut down, the Roost's largely delivery-oriented model was able to remain open the entire pandemic."

— Inuvik Drum, August 18, 2022

That's the thing about The Roost. It was built for takeout before takeout was a strategy — a fry-up counter and a backroom where the town eats together. When the world closed, the kitchen kept cooking: masks on, backroom shut, delivery running. Inuvik noticed, and Inuvik remembers.

The kitchen is run by Moody Elkhatib, who told the Drum the formula plainly: homemade pizza sauce, garlic sauce from scratch, fresh dough made daily — and best sellers that never left the board: shawarma, donairs, spaghetti, pizza, lasagne.

The timeline

Years the grill
didn't go out

2012

The backroom gets rebuilt

New stain on the walls, new flooring, new benches on the way — co-owner Terry El-Khatib refreshes the back dining room. The room where half of Inuvik has eaten a donair. (Inuvik Drum, Dec 2012)

2020–22

Open through the whole pandemic

Delivery-oriented from day one, the kitchen stays open the entire COVID-19 pandemic while much of the industry closes. Masks mandatory, backroom shut, food still going out the door. (Inuvik Drum, Aug 2022)

2022

The nine-patty burger contest

On August 20, two contestants face a massive nine-patty burger with deep-fried sides — spuds, deep-fried cheese sticks, onion rings. Finish it and win a $150 Booza Box; lose and the meal's value goes to a charity of your choice. Peak Roost. (Inuvik Drum, Aug 2022)

Today

Still the late-night kitchen

Burgers, pizza, donair, shawarma, Chinese, fish and chips — open til midnight, seven days. The backroom is open again. The grill never really cooled down.
